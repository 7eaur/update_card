# UPDATE CARD — Service Catalog & Information Architecture

**Status:** DRAFT / USER-SUPPLIED BASELINE  
**Purpose:** Organize the currently known service catalog and prepare it for website information architecture.  
**Important:** This document is intentionally separate from the general web design/engineering standard. It describes the UPDATE CARD business/service content, not universal design rules.

---

## 1. Current product understanding

UPDATE CARD is being planned as a **multi-page informational / company profile website**, not an online store.

The website should help a visitor:

1. Understand who UPDATE CARD is.
2. Discover the main areas of service.
3. Explore detailed service information on dedicated pages.
4. Understand how the business can help.
5. Contact the business through the published contact channel when ready.

The website should **not** be designed as a catalog checkout experience unless the business model changes later.

That means the content model should prioritize explanation, discovery, clarity, trust, and contact rather than carts, checkout, product inventory, or account features.

# 4. Why the taxonomy was adjusted

The supplied content is useful, but three areas need separation for a clearer website:

1. **Subscriptions vs software licenses**  
   A Netflix subscription and a Windows/Office license are different customer intents and should not be forced into the same page structure.

2. **Electronic payments vs international purchasing**  
   Paying a website or handling USDT is not the same journey as buying a physical product abroad and delivering it to Yemen.

3. **Social communication vs social entertainment apps**  
   Several listed platforms are entertainment/live/social-game products. A broader category name avoids misleading users.

This creates a cleaner current architecture of **7 main service families**:

1. Games
2. Social & entertainment apps
3. Gift cards
4. Subscriptions
5. Software & licenses
6. Electronic payments & digital assets
7. International purchasing & delivery

This is an IA draft, not a final routing contract.

```text
├── about/
├── services/
│   ├── games/
│   ├── social-entertainment-apps/
│   ├── gift-cards/
│   ├── subscriptions/
│   ├── software-licenses/
│   ├── electronic-payments-digital-assets/
│   └── international-shopping-delivery/
├── how-it-works/
├── faq/
└── contact/
```

### Important

Do **not** create one page per game, card, app, or brand by default.

A dedicated child page is justified only when at least one of these is true:

- there is enough useful content to explain,
- the customer journey is materially different,
- there are important regional/compatibility rules,
- there is meaningful SEO value,
- or the service is important enough to deserve a dedicated landing page.

This prevents a large collection of thin, repetitive pages.

# 6. Suggested content model for each service family

Every main service page should be able to answer:

1. What is this service family?
2. What kinds of services are available within it?
3. Which brands/platforms are currently supported?
4. What does the customer typically need to provide?
5. Are there region/account/platform restrictions?
6. Is availability fixed or variable?
7. How does the customer continue/contact?
8. What common questions need clarification?

Do not force every page into identical cards if the content type is different.

# 10. Current conclusion

The service offering is broader than “game recharge”. UPDATE CARD currently appears to span three larger customer needs:

1. **Digital entertainment & digital access**  
   Games, social/entertainment apps, gift cards, subscriptions, software/licenses.

2. **Digital payment enablement**  
   Digital assets, wallets/platforms, PayPal/card-based or website payment services.

3. **International purchase enablement**  
   Purchasing from international stores and delivery to Yemen.

The website information architecture should communicate this breadth without turning the experience into an e-commerce store.

The home page should summarize these areas; detailed pages should explain them progressively.
